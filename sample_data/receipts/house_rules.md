## Who is charged for what

1. **Groceries (Kettle & Crate):** split everything three ways. We all eat it, and that includes the non-food item on that receipt (the aluminum foil).
2. **Pharmacy (Lindenwick):** personal items go to their owner.
   - Shampoo: **Alex**
   - Contact lens solution: **Jordan**

   - Paper towels, cold/flu caplets (for the house medicine cabinet), and facial tissues are shared. Split them three ways.
3. **Wine & spirits (Talloway):** Alex doesn't drink. All alcohol (the wine, beer, and tequila) is split between **Sam and Jordan** only. Everything non-alcoholic on that receipt (seltzer, chips, party cups) is split three ways.
4. **Pizza night (Lanternfly):** Alex was away that night. Split the whole order between **Sam and Jordan**, including tax and tip.
5. **Hardware / household (Ironbark):** split everything three ways.

## How to do the math

- **Items:** if an item is shared by *k* people, each person is charged `line_total / k`.
- **Tax:** use the tax line printed on the receipt. Split each tax line in proportion to how much of *that line's taxed items* each person is charged for. Untaxed items (groceries, package-store alcohol) carry no tax. Items marked `T` are taxed at 6.25%, and items marked `M` carry the 7% meals tax.
- **Tip:** split in proportion to each person's share of that receipt's pre-tax subtotal.
- **Rounding:** work out exact amounts first. Then, for each receipt, round every **non-payer's** share to the nearest cent (half-up). The **payer's** share is the receipt total minus the others' rounded shares, so the payer absorbs any leftover cent.
- **Balances:** a person's net balance is total paid minus total share. A positive balance means they are owed money, and the balances across the house must sum to $0.00.
- **Settling up:** settle with the fewest possible transfers. People who owe pay people who are owed.
